03Urban Flood Nowcasting System

Design Thinking Review · Team of four · B.E. CSE (I Year), Section E · RMD Engineering College

Cities often flood not only because of heavy rain, but because local drainage cannot clear the water fast enough. Our team proposed a decision-support prototype that combines rainfall data, drainage conditions and geotagged citizen reports to estimate short-term, location-specific flood risk. A transparent rule-based approach assigns Low, Moderate or High risk, shown on a map-based dashboard so municipal staff can decide where to inspect first.

Collect→Estimate→Review and act

PROBLEM AND GAPS

  • Rainfall alone does not explain local flooding, because drain condition is ignored.
  • Rainfall, complaints and citizen reports sit in separate sources.
  • Blocked or damaged drains are rarely tracked by location, and there is no early way to prioritise inspections.

WHAT WE DID

  • Reviewed five research papers on rainfall nowcasting, flood prediction and crowdsourced flood monitoring.
  • Designed the three-stage flow and a structured citizen-report format with verification status.
  • Defined Low / Moderate / High risk rules and created an authority dashboard mock-up with demo data.

MY ROLE

Contributed to the group presentation by helping introduce the topic and communicate key ideas.

Design ThinkingProblem AnalysisLiterature ReviewDashboard DesignTeamwork

Phase I (design and concept) is complete. Phase II (live data, calibration and validation) is planned. All data shown is illustrative demo data, and forecast accuracy is not yet validated. It supports, and does not replace, official flood warnings.

Team: Tharun Karthik A, Udhayakumar, Suyash and Sri Manigandan.
Guided by: Dr. T. A. Sundaravadivel and Ms. Evelin Jessy, RMD Engineering College.

04What Happens Inside a Computer When You Play a Video Game?

Technical Presentation · Presented by me · B.E. CSE, Section E

A game is a real-time system where many components work together almost instantly. This presentation explains how a button press becomes a virtual world on screen, and why every part of the computer matters for smooth gameplay.

Player input→CPU→GPU→Display

TOPICS COVERED

  • CPU: the game's coordinator, handling game logic, movement, collisions and rules.
  • GPU: the image creator, rendering 3D models, lighting, shadows and frames.
  • RAM vs. storage: RAM is your desk (temporary working memory); storage is your bookshelf (long-term game files).
  • Cooling: fans, heat sinks and thermal control keep performance stable under load.
  • FPS: frames per second, from about 33 ms per frame at 30 FPS to about 8 ms at 120 FPS.
  • Bottlenecks: performance depends on the whole system, not just one powerful part.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Gaming is an example of many engineering systems (code, computation, memory, graphics and thermal engineering) working together.
